Your agent pays for APIs or services on Arc Mainnet. Before each payment it buys one
trust check from A-Identity, paid in USDC through Circle Gateway nanopayments (the buyer
signs, nobody pays gas, Circle batches the settlement), and acts on the answer. This page
is the whole integration. Every endpoint, header, price and address on it was read from
the running service and from Circle's own endpoints on 2026-11-04, except the
[verifyThenPay section](#verify-then-pay-from-a-vault), which is dated on its own. The ten
minutes start once your wallet holds USDC on Arc Mainnet; [getting it there](#what-you-need)
is the slow part for most teams.

**Your team pays for every check from its own wallet and its own Gateway balance. We do not
fund your checks and we never hold your key. Every check you pay shows on
[/proof/arc](https://a-identity.xyz/proof/arc) as external.** That is what Milestone 2 of the
Arc Acceleration Season asks of an outside team: buy a check before you pay on Arc Mainnet.
[`verifyThenPay`](#verify-then-pay-from-a-vault) is the next step, and its hosted route and MCP
tools are live today, and its SDK is on npm.

## What you get

- **A verdict before the payment.** `risk_check` returns `decision` (`ALLOW`, `WARN` or
  `DENY`), a `risk` band and `reasons` as plain sentences, for the payee and the amount
  you pass. 0.005 USDC per call. `counterparty_check` (0.008 USDC) does the same for the
  pair payer plus payee and also detects same-operator self-dealing.
- **The cheapest check.** `verify_agent` at 0.001 USDC answers whether the payee holds an
  ERC-8004 identity, its KYA status and whether its endpoint is live. It is identity, not
  a verdict: the verdict tools start at 0.005 USDC.
- **What a check reads.** The ERC-8004 registry, live on-chain (owner and registration
  URI), the KYA wallet-control attestation, a deterministic 0 to 1000 reputation computed
  from settled payments, validation and tenure with a Sybil check, whether the agent is
  revoked, and the `txContext.amountUsd` you pass, because a payee that is fine for one
  dollar may not be fine for ten thousand. Nothing in a verdict is a language-model guess.

The free preview is coarse on purpose: `GET /api/x402/gateway/tools/trust_preview?agentId=...`
returns a band and flags, no verdict and no reasons, and is rate limited.

## What you need

<Steps>
  <Step title="An Arc Mainnet wallet holding USDC">
    Arc Mainnet is `eip155:5042` (chain id 5042), RPC `https://rpc.mainnet.arc.io`, no
    API key. Gas on Arc is USDC itself, so keep a little USDC beyond what you deposit.
    The payer address is the one that will appear on our proof page, so use a wallet
    you are happy to be attributed by. With the SDK or the reference script that is your
    key's own address. With a Circle CLI agent wallet it is the wallet's backing EOA, not the
    `--address` you pass, so that is the address to send us.

    **Getting USDC onto Arc Mainnet.** We do not supply it, and this is usually the first
    thing that stops a team. What we know, read on 2026-11-04:

    - The route is Circle's Cross-Chain Transfer Protocol (CCTP), which
      [Arc names as its canonical bridge](https://docs.arc.io/integrate/infrastructure/bridges):
      USDC is burned on the source chain and minted natively on Arc, CCTP domain `26`.
    - [Arc's Get USDC section](https://docs.arc.io/arc-chain#get-usdc) lists the USDC Bridge
      app at `bridge.usdc.com`, Bridge Kit for doing it from code, and
      [USDC providers](https://www.usdc.com/providers) for buying USDC. Our proof log holds no
      transfer through that app and no purchase from a provider, so we cannot tell you their
      fees or timing.
    - What we have done ourselves is a CCTP V2 burn on Base that Circle's Forwarding Service
      minted on Arc Mainnet; the receipts are on [/proof/arc](https://a-identity.xyz/proof/arc).
      A plain CCTP transfer ends with a `receiveMessage` call on Arc, and a wallet with no USDC
      on Arc cannot pay the gas for it. The
      [Forwarding Service](https://docs.arc.io/app-kit/tutorials/bridge/use-forwarding-service)
      submits that mint for you and takes its fee from the transfer.

    <Warning>
      Fake bridge sites are a common way to lose a wallet. Open a bridge only from the link on
      [Arc's docs](https://docs.arc.io/arc-chain#get-usdc), never from a search ad or a message,
      and that includes a message that claims to come from us. Read what your wallet asks you to
      sign, and send a small amount first. No bridge, and nobody from A-Identity, needs your seed
      phrase or private key. USDC on Arc Mainnet is only the contract in the table below: Arc has
      no wrapped USDC, so a token offered as wrapped USDC on Arc is not it.
    </Warning>

    Bring a little more than you plan to deposit. The code below deposits 1 USDC, which is 200
    `risk_check` calls, and the Circle CLI refuses a Gateway deposit under 0.5 USDC.
  </Step>

    on-chain transaction pair: `approve` the GatewayWallet on the USDC contract, then call
    `deposit(token, value)` on the GatewayWallet. Circle credits it after about one block
    (its supported-blockchains table lists Arc at about 0.5 seconds). 1 USDC covers 1000
    `verify_agent` calls or 200 `risk_check` calls.

    - **`@circle-fin/x402-batching`**, the one to use if your team already holds a key:

## The 10-minute flow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Call the tool and read the 402">
    Every paid tool answers `GET` (query parameters) and `POST` (JSON body). Unpaid, both
    answer `402` with the requirements in the body and, base64-encoded, in the
    `PAYMENT-REQUIRED` header; the two are identical. This is the live challenge:

    ```bash
    curl -s -D - 'https://a-identity.xyz/api/x402/gateway/tools/risk_check?agentId=0x4e26Ae66840134Ac5979c56ba2509F3956b93431&amountUsd=25'
    ```

    other assets in `extra.assets` and some extra fields in `accepts` and `tool` are cut; the call
    above returns all of it:

    `amount` is in USDC base units (6 decimals): `5000` is 0.005 USDC. `settlementFeeUsd`
    is 0 on this rail; the price is the whole cost. `domainVerified` and `provenAt` say that
    before this challenge was served we read Gateway's live `/v1/x402/supported` and found
    the same `verifyingContract` and the same USDC the registry expects. Pick the
    `eip155:5042` entry.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pay">
    Sign an EIP-3009 authorization against the `GatewayWalletBatched` domain (name
    `GatewayWalletBatched`, version `1`, chainId `5042`, verifyingContract
    `0x77777777Dcc4d5A8B6E418Fd04D8997ef11000eE`) for exactly `amount`, to `payTo`, and
    retry the same request with the signed payload. Any of the three clients does this
    for you. With a key your team already holds, use the `@circle-fin/x402-batching` tab:
    the Circle CLI pays only from a wallet you log in to with `circle wallet login`, and a
    key added with `circle wallet import` cannot run `circle services pay`.

    From an empty folder, on Node 24, the SDK path is:

    ```bash
    npm init -y && npm pkg set type=module
    npm install @circle-fin/x402-batching
    # save the first tab below as check.ts, then, with a key that holds USDC on Arc Mainnet:
    BUYER_KEY=0x... node check.ts
    ```

    <CodeGroup>
    ```ts @circle-fin/x402-batching

      chain: 'arc',                                 // Arc Mainnet, Gateway domain 26

      rpcUrl: 'https://rpc.mainnet.arc.io',         // optional on 4.x, required on 3.x

    await gateway.deposit('1')                      // once, then remove: approve + deposit on the GatewayWallet

    const { data } = await gateway.pay('https://a-identity.xyz/api/x402/gateway/tools/risk_check', {
      method: 'POST',

    ```

    The script sends `agentId` only; pass `txContext` through the CLI or the SDK. If you
    write your own client, the retry carries one header, `PAYMENT-SIGNATURE`, whose value
    is base64 of this JSON (the older `X-PAYMENT` name is accepted too):

    ```json
    {
      "x402Version": 2,
      "accepted": "the accepts entry you chose, unchanged",
      "payload": {
        "signature": "0x...",
        "authorization": {
          "from": "0xYourWallet",
          "to": "0xd305607510e0db2c95807173c7a05bea53c1ed36",
          "value": "5000",

          "nonce": "0x<32 random bytes>"
    ```

    `value` must be exactly the price and `to` exactly our `payTo`; anything else is
    refused with a fresh 402 and nothing is settled.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the verdict">
    A paid call answers `200` with the tool result plus a `settlement` record, and a
    `PAYMENT-RESPONSE` header (base64 JSON: `success`, `transaction`, `network`, `payer`,
    `amount`). The shape, with illustrative values for the verdict fields:

    ```json

      "reasons": ["Moderate reputation (470): not enough for us to vouch for it"],

      "_meta": { "network": "Arc Mainnet (eip155:5042)", "proof": "/api/x402/gateway/proof" },
      "settlement": {
        "ts": "2026-09-24T16:43:58.731Z",
        "outcome": "completed",
        "tool": "verify_agent",
        "network": "eip155:5042",
        "assetSymbol": "USDC",
        "value": "1000",
        "amountUsd": 0.001,
        "payer": "0x8c8d9cd12d8896a40cf2115ee731258bb4983349",
        "payTo": "0xd305607510e0db2c95807173c7a05bea53c1ed36",
        "authNonce": "0xd232cc9d...a709d8481",
        "transferId": "e06f2175-132f-4f5f-af3f-04ed42b9a028",
        "gatewayStatus": "completed",
        "confirmedBy": "gateway-transfers-api",
        "tx": "0x1338762a8203452d75dfeea76a33fa296c299c6e9fc297bec9112bbe801e0413",
        "explorerUrl": "https://explorer.arc.io/tx/0x1338762a8203452d75dfeea76a33fa296c299c6e9fc297bec9112bbe801e0413"
      }
    }
    ```

    `decision` is `ALLOW`, `WARN` or `DENY`. On `DENY`, do not make the payment. `WARN` is not
    a go-ahead: hold the payment for a person, which is what `verifyThenPay` does by itself. The
    `settlement` block above is a real row from the proof log (a `verify_agent` call our own
    wallet paid on 2026-09-24, labeled internal there); yours carries your payer and your
    tool. A fresh call comes back as `credited` with no `tx` until Gateway's on-chain batch
    lands, and the proof page upgrades the row to `completed` with the batch hash when it does.

    | Status | Meaning |
    | --- | --- |
    | `400` | A required input is missing. Checked before any payment is taken. |
    | `402` | No header, a malformed header, `to` not our `payTo`, `value` not exactly the price, or Gateway refused. The body is the challenge again plus `verifyError`. |
    | `503` | Gateway's kind for Arc could not be proven against the registry right now. No challenge is served rather than one nobody could settle. |
    | `502` | Gateway answered success but its transfers API returned no matching transfer inside the window, or the settle call itself got no usable answer. Whether money moved is not known, so no challenge is served: do not sign again before looking the nonce up. Nothing served, nothing counted; the row is recorded as `unconfirmed` on the proof so it can be resolved. |
    | `500` | Credited and recorded, but the tool failed on our side. The body names the transfer. |
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The tools

Six paid tools and one free preview, at `https://a-identity.xyz/api/x402/gateway/tools/<name>`.
`agentId`, `from` and `to` each accept a platform agent id, an ERC-8004 token id (`#73232`), a
CAIP id (`eip155:<chain>:8004/<token>`) or a 0x address; the next paragraph says what each form
gets you. `txContext` is optional and takes
`{ "amountUsd": number, "payee": "0x address" }`; on `GET`, pass it as a JSON string or use
`amountUsd=25` as a shortcut. Prices are the ones the 402 charges; the OpenAPI document at
[/api/x402/gateway/openapi.json](https://a-identity.xyz/api/x402/gateway/openapi.json) is
generated from the same source.

**Send the payee's 0x address as `agentId`.** It needs no chain read. `risk_check` also takes a
platform id, a token id, a CAIP token id and a CAIP-10 account, and they reach the same record: a token
answers on the chain it names, or through the record that proved the address holding it. A token we
hold no record of gets `WARN`. An id that reaches no record and no identity was never looked up, so it
stays strict: `DENY`, with reasons that say we could not look and hold no evidence, and to ask again
with the 0x address.

**Who gets what.** `DENY` means we hold evidence against the payee: KYA revoked, Sybil high, or, on an
agent on our platform whose record holds rejected settlements or guardrail override attempts, a
reputation under 200 or a payment over 100 USD with reputation under 400. It also comes out when we could
not read our record and stayed strict, and the reasons say which. A contested job lowers the score and is
named in the reasons, and it is not evidence by itself: it costs the client nothing while we fund the
escrow, so counting it would let a stranger write a `DENY` on a vendor. A low score alone
is not evidence: an agent on our platform with nothing against it gets `WARN` at those lines, with
reasons that say our record holds nothing against it and not enough to vouch for it, and the amount
named when it is over 100 USD. A payee with no on-chain identity, or with an identity and no record on
our platform, is not known bad, so it gets `WARN`, with reasons that say what we do not know.
A high-value or large payment to such a payee stays `WARN`, with the amount named. `ALLOW` needs
a platform agent with reputation of at least 500, attested KYA and at least 7 days of tenure.
A real seller who simply has not registered with us scores at most 60 because we have no history
for it, and that gets `WARN`, and signing up with us does not make that worse. Until 2026-11-04 it got `DENY`; anything dated before that day on
this page or on [/proof/arc](https://a-identity.xyz/proof/arc) was answered under the older rule.

**What that line cannot see.** `DENY` binds an address we hold a proven record of. A record counts
at an address only once it proved control of it, so a registration that merely names a wallet does
not count. A payee with a bad record can appear under a new address and get `WARN`, so `WARN` means
unknown, not clean. Read the reasons before you pay on one.

| Tool | Price | What it answers | Required input |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `verify_agent` | 0.001 USDC | ERC-8004 identity, KYA status, endpoint liveness | `agentId` |
| `reputation_score` | 0.002 USDC | Deterministic 0 to 1000 reputation with a full breakdown and its on-chain attestation | `agentId` |
| `risk_check` | 0.005 USDC | Pre-transaction ALLOW / WARN / DENY verdict on a counterparty, with reasons | `agentId` (`txContext` optional) |
| `guardrail_check` | 0.005 USDC | Whether the agent operates under an enforced spend or trade policy and respects verdicts; bands only, no caps, allowlists, symbols, amounts or holdings disclosed | `agentId` |
| `counterparty_check` | 0.008 USDC | Deal-specific verdict between two agents: counterparty risk plus same-operator self-deal detection | `from`, `to` (`txContext` optional) |
| `agent_passport` | 0.01 USDC | The full passport: identity, KYA, reputation and risk in one call | `agentId` |
| `trust_preview` | free, rate limited | Coarse trust band, flags and the price list; `GET` only | `agentId` |

The same tools, at the same prices, are sold on Base (`eip155:8453`) through the same
rail; the 402 lists both and you pick by `network`.

## Where your payments show up

Your wallet pays, from its own Gateway balance, and it is labeled **external** automatically:
our own buyer wallets are on the rail's published internal list, and every other payer is external. Nothing is counted until the
transfer has been read back from Gateway's transfers API for the authorization's nonce,
to our `payTo`, for the price.

- [a-identity.xyz/proof/arc](https://a-identity.xyz/proof/arc) shows the totals for Arc
  Mainnet and the newest eight settled checks, each with the tool, the amount, "via Circle
  Gateway" and the batch transaction once it lands. Rows paid by our wallets carry an
  "internal" tag; yours carry none. The counter reads "Circle Gateway internal / external
  (all networks)".
- [/api/x402/gateway/proof](https://a-identity.xyz/api/x402/gateway/proof) is the JSON
  behind it: `internalSettlements`, `externalSettlements`, the `internalPayers` list and
  the last 50 rows with their `payer`.
- `GET https://a-identity.xyz/api/arc/season` is the Arc Acceleration Season report,
  computed from the same logs on every request: `usage.internal` and `usage.external`,
  external usage per wallet (`checks`, `usd`, first and last payment, `activeDays`), and
  the Milestone 2 counts against their targets (external teams live, 30 external checks,
  a team active on more than one day, a publicly referenceable integration). Each owner-held
  vault under `milestone1.vaults.items` lists the freezes and withdrawals its owner's wallet
  signed, with hashes, and `actionsRead` says how far the chain was read.
- `POST https://a-identity.xyz/api/arc/vaults` with `{"deployTx": "0x..."}` admits an
  owner-held vault on Arc Mainnet by its deploy transaction, or refuses with the reasons. It
  signs nothing, needs no session, and the same hash twice is a no-op. The vault page at
  `/app/vault/arc` does this for you. `GET` on the same path lists the admitted vaults.

Send your payer address and your team name to
[hello@a-identity.xyz](mailto:hello@a-identity.xyz) so the usage is attributed to you by
name, and say whether we may reference the integration in public. That attribution is a
list we keep by hand, and a team is added only after its wallet has paid a check; the
external label itself needs nothing from you.

## Verify, then pay from a vault

`verifyThenPay` puts the check and the payment in one flow on Arc Mainnet. An
[AgentSpendPolicy](/chains/arc) vault pays, its limits hold on chain, and the check is bought
first, for 0.005 USDC, from a Gateway balance. Checked on 2026-11-04:

| Path | Status |
| --- | --- |
| Hosted routes | **Live.** They validate input and serve flow records. 100 flows have run on Arc Mainnet, all through our own launch-day vault to payees of ours and labeled so: 75 `ALLOW` flows that the vault paid, and 25 `DENY` flows stopped before signing, answered on 2026-11-01 and 2026-11-02 UTC under the rule of that day. Each is listed on [/proof/arc](https://a-identity.xyz/proof/arc). |
| MCP tools | **Live** on `https://a-identity.xyz/mcp`, which lists both. The stdio server does not carry them. |
| SDK, `@a-identity/trust-guard` 0.4.0 | **On npm**, with the `/arc` entry point. This code ran our own 100 flows. No outside team has run the published package yet. |

<Note>
A vault payment is a direct USDC transfer to the checked payee, not an x402 settlement. The vault
cannot settle an x402 offer: its agent payment path is `pay(to, amount)`, sent plainly, with no
Memo. So a payment names no check; the server links them by vault, payee, amount and block order.
</Note>

**Whose keys.** A vault you deploy at [/app/vault/arc](https://a-identity.xyz/app/vault/arc) is
yours: your wallet is the owner, your own agent is the operator, and A-Identity holds neither key.
The check's payer defaults to the operator and can be a separate key. Our own flows run
through our launch-day vault `0xe07819e6d28a5dfd5a9f4cd94dc6dc9aeeda4146`, whose owner is our
operating wallet and whose operator is our env-gated key, so they are not owner-held and are
labeled internal.

### What it checks, in order

<Steps>
  <Step title="Free checks, nothing signed">
    The asset is the registry's USDC and the vault's. An agent named in `payeeAgentId` is the
    payee's. The vault's policy and a simulated `pay()` from its operator accept the payment (not
    frozen, session key valid, payee allowed, within auto-approve, daily cap and balance). The
    operator can pay the gas. If the vault would refuse, the answer is a refusal named after the
    vault's own error, and the check is not bought. A payee that holds no agent is not refused.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The check, 0.005 USDC">
    `risk_check` through Circle Gateway, with the payee's address as `agentId` and the amount in
    `txContext`. Never a CAIP id. It is the same settlement row as a direct `risk_check`, so your
    wallet shows on [/proof/arc](https://a-identity.xyz/proof/arc) as external here too.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The decision">
    Read from the table below. Anything that is not a usable verdict counts as `DENY`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The payment, from your key">
    The caller sends the prepared call with its own key. Nothing is retried with a new nonce.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The record">
    The transaction hash is posted to the flow. The server reads the receipt itself and records
    only what it proves.
  </Step>
</Steps>

| Verdict | What happens | Paid by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `DENY` | Nothing to sign. The refusal is public on the flow record. You have paid for the check and nothing else. | Nobody |
| `WARN` | No automatic payment. The answer is the verdict plus a prepared `ownerPay` for the vault's owner (`awaiting_human`). Set `payOnWarn` to true to prepare `pay()` for the operator instead; it is off unless you ask. A payee we know nothing about gets `WARN`, so `payOnWarn` on such a payee is your own explicit choice to pay it. | The owner, or the operator with `payOnWarn` |
| `ALLOW` | The exact `pay(payee, amount)` for the operator to send. | The operator |

A payee that holds no agent on Arc Mainnet passes the free checks, within the vault's ceiling and
allowlist, and the check is then bought for 0.005 USDC. Unless it is an agent on our platform, the
answer is `WARN`, so it reaches `awaiting_human` with the owner's prepared payment. The same goes
for one that holds an agent but has no record on our platform. On a vault whose owner and operator
are one key, that `WARN` stops at `warn_no_human_gate` instead, with the check paid and nothing
prepared. An agent named in `payeeAgentId` that the payee does not own
(`PAYEE_AGENT_MISMATCH`) and a registry that does not answer (`REGISTRY_UNREADABLE`) are still
refused at the free checks, with nothing bought.

**The vault's own limits come first.** A payment above the vault's per-payment ceiling
(`AboveAutoApprove`; a vault deployed at [/app/vault/arc](https://a-identity.xyz/app/vault/arc)
starts at 0.25 USDC), or to a payee off an allowlist that is on (`PayeeNotAllowed`), is refused at
the free checks. Nothing is bought, no verdict comes back and nothing is prepared. Only the owner
can pay it as it stands, by calling `ownerPay` directly, with no verdict behind it. For a verdict
and a hand-off, the owner raises the ceiling or allows the payee, and the operator asks again.

### When a payment waits for its owner

`awaiting_human` hands back the exact call for the owner's key, and that key has to send it. The
vault page does not do that today: it has no action for sending `ownerPay` or posting its receipt. If
you deployed from the vault page your wallet is the owner, so you can send it from that wallet, with
no key leaving it, using the sample below in the browser console or your own page. Read the verdict's
reasons first.

The flow id is what your agent got back: `out.flowId` from `verifyThenPay`, or `flowId` from the hosted
route. Have it hand that to you. There is no list of waiting flows by vault. The flow record is public,
so nothing here needs a secret, and the sample encodes the call itself rather than trusting the record.

```ts
import { createPublicClient, createWalletClient, custom, encodeFunctionData, http, parseAbi } from 'viem'
import { ARC_MAINNET, arcViemChain } from '@a-identity/trust-guard/arc'

const base = 'https://a-identity.xyz/api/x402/gateway'
const { flow } = await (await fetch(`${base}/flows/${flowId}`)).json()
if (flow?.outcome !== 'awaiting_human') throw new Error('this flow is not waiting for its owner')
console.log(flow.verdict.reasons) // read these before you pay

const abi = parseAbi(['function ownerPay(address to, uint256 amount)'])
const args = [flow.payee, BigInt(flow.amount)] as const
const data = encodeFunctionData({ abi, functionName: 'ownerPay', args })
if (data !== flow.prepared.data) throw new Error('the record prepared a different call than the one it describes')

const [owner] = await window.ethereum.request({ method: 'eth_requestAccounts' })
const reader = createPublicClient({ chain: arcViemChain, transport: http() })
// Throws if the vault would refuse it right now: sending it then would revert and cost gas.
await reader.simulateContract({ account: owner, address: flow.vault, abi, functionName: 'ownerPay', args })

const wallet = createWalletClient({ account: owner, chain: arcViemChain, transport: custom(window.ethereum) })
const txHash = await wallet.sendTransaction({
  to: flow.vault,
  data,
  value: 0n,
  maxFeePerGas: ARC_MAINNET.minMaxFeePerGasWei * 2n,
  maxPriorityFeePerGas: 1_000_000_000n,
})
await fetch(`${base}/flows/${flowId}/receipt`, {
  method: 'POST',
  headers: { 'content-type': 'application/json' },
  body: JSON.stringify({ txHash }),
})
```

A script holding the owner key does the same with `privateKeyToAccount` in place of `custom(window.ethereum)`,
and with what `verifyThenPay` returned in `out.ownerPay` when it runs in the owner's process. Do not put a
browser wallet's key into a file to do this. Over the hosted route the same call is
`ownerHandoff.ownerPay`, with its fee under `fees`; send it, then POST the hash to `/flows/<flowId>/receipt`.

### The hosted route

`POST https://a-identity.xyz/api/x402/gateway/verify-then-pay` takes the vault, the payee and the
amount in USDC base units (6 decimals, digits only). It never signs and never broadcasts. The
answer is always `executed: false`.

```bash
curl -s -X POST https://a-identity.xyz/api/x402/gateway/verify-then-pay \
  -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"vault":"0xYourVault","payee":"0x4e26Ae66840134Ac5979c56ba2509F3956b93431","amount":"250000"}'
```

Unpaid, it answers `402` with the Arc Mainnet offer in `challenge`. Sign it as in
[Pay](#the-10-minute-flow), from your Gateway balance, and send the same body again with the
`PAYMENT-SIGNATURE` header. Optional fields are `asset`, `payeeAgentId` (checked on chain only, and
never sent to `risk_check`) and `payOnWarn`. The body's `stage` says where the flow stands:

| Status | `stage` | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `400`, `422` | `refused` | Bad input, a named agent that is not the payee's, or the vault would refuse. `code` names why. Nothing charged, and no verdict comes back. |
| `409` | `refused` | `NONCE_ALREADY_USED`: this authorization already opened a flow. This request charged nothing, but the first request with this nonce may have. Read `GET /api/x402/gateway/flows/<nonce>` or the Gateway proof log before signing a new one. |
| `402` | `payment_required` | The free checks passed. Nothing charged yet. A payee that holds no agent passes them, with `checks.payee.agentsHeld` 0, and `next` says what to expect. |
| `200` | `prepared` | Send `prepared` from the operator key, unless `prepared.simulation.ok` is false, which means the vault would refuse it right now. |
| `200` | `awaiting_human` | `WARN`: send `ownerHandoff.ownerPay` from the owner key, unless `ownerHandoff.ownerPay.simulation.ok` is false, which means the vault would refuse it right now. A payee we know nothing about ends here. |
| `200` | `deny`, `verdict_unusable` | Do not pay. Nothing was prepared. |
| `200` | `warn_no_human_gate` | `WARN`, and the vault's owner and operator are one key, so there is nobody to hand it to. The check was paid. Do not pay. Nothing was prepared. |
| `409`, `500` | `deny`, `verdict_unusable` | Do not pay. The check was paid and answered, but the flow log does not hold the refusal, so it is not public. |
| `429` | `refused` | Over the budget of 30 calls a minute per address. Nothing charged. |
| `503` | `refused` | The chain or the flow log did not answer, so nothing could be decided. Nothing charged. Ask again shortly. |
| `409`, `500`, `502`, `503` | `check_failed`, `unavailable` | No payment to send. `charged` says whether the check was paid, and `null` means that is not known: do not sign again before looking the nonce up. |
| `501` | `unavailable` | The rail is not selling on Arc Mainnet right now. Nothing charged. |

If you send the transaction yourself, three things about Arc matter. A `maxFeePerGas` below 20 gwei
is dropped silently, with no error. One RPC can answer null for a receipt that exists, so a null
does not mean the transaction is gone; never resend it with a new nonce. And USDC has 6 decimals as
an ERC-20 but 18 as native gas, so the amount here is always the 6-decimal one.

### The MCP tools

`verify_then_pay` and `verify_then_pay_receipt` are these routes through the
[MCP server](/developers/mcp-server). They never sign. Call `verify_then_pay` without `payment` to
get the challenge, then again with `payment` set to the base64 signature your own key made. Then
send the prepared call yourself and pass the hash to `verify_then_pay_receipt`.

### The public flow record

`GET https://a-identity.xyz/api/x402/gateway/flows/<flowId>` needs no key. The `flowId` is the
check's authorization nonce. The record holds the vault with its owner and operator, the payee, the
amount, the check, the verdict with its reasons, the prepared call, and the payment once proven.
It is public on purpose, for every flow, because a `DENY` has no transaction and this record is the
evidence it happened. Do not use this route for a payment you need to keep private.

`POST .../flows/<flowId>/receipt` with `{"txHash":"0x..."}` closes a flow. The server reads the
receipt from every RPC and marks the flow `executed` only on the vault's `Paid` event and USDC's
`Transfer` to the checked payee for the checked amount, in a block after the verdict. A flow waiting
for its owner is closed only by the owner's call.

### The SDK

`verifyThenPay` from `@a-identity/trust-guard/arc` runs all of this with your keys: it encodes the
payment from your own inputs, compares it byte for byte with what the server prepared, sends it, and
posts the receipt. A compromised server cannot redirect the payment. The verdict itself is not
signed, so you still trust our server and the TLS connection for what it says.

Install it with `npm install @a-identity/trust-guard viem @circle-fin/x402-batching`, version 0.4.0
or later. Its [README](https://github.com/getA-Identity/A-Identity/blob/main/trust-guard/README.md)
has the code and every error it throws. For Milestone 2, the direct check at the top of this page is
the shortest path.

## Free reads

Identity and reputation reads cost nothing and need no key. The MCP server at
`https://a-identity.xyz/mcp` (Streamable HTTP, stateless) exposes them to any MCP client:

```bash
claude mcp add --transport http a-identity https://a-identity.xyz/mcp
```

`resolve_agent` reads an ERC-8004 identity live from the registry, `get_reputation`
returns the deterministic score with its breakdown, `merchant_check` returns a free
ALLOW / WARN / DENY for a merchant-side agent before a checkout, and `get_chain_status`
lists every chain in the registry with its live status. The free `trust_preview` on this rail is the same
idea over plain HTTP. Free reads do not appear on the proof page, because there is no
payment to record, and they do not count as usage for Milestone 2, which counts paid checks only.
The full tool list is on the [MCP server](/developers/mcp-server) page.

Two free reads need the chain named. Without `chain`, `evm_pay_check` reads a bare address, a `#0`
id or a link on Robinhood Chain, so pass `chain: "arc-mainnet"` for those; a full id such as
`eip155:5042:8004/0` names its own chain. `resolve_agent` with a bare token id
such as `#0` can match agents on several chains, so pass the full id, `eip155:5042:8004/0`.

## What we never do

- **No keys of yours on our servers.** You sign locally, in the CLI, the SDK or your own
  code; we receive a signed authorization that can move exactly the price to exactly our
  `payTo`, and nothing else.
- **No payment counted without a Gateway read-back.** Gateway's settle response saying
  success is not enough: the transfer must come back from Gateway's transfers API for your
  nonce, to our `payTo`, for the price, before the result is served or the row is recorded
  as revenue. A read-back that fails is a 502 and an `unconfirmed` row in public.
- **No challenge we could not settle.** Before any 402 we read Gateway's live
  `/v1/x402/supported` and refuse to sell if the advertised `verifyingContract` or USDC
  differs from the registry.
- **Internal traffic labeled, never hidden.** Our own payments are on the same proof page
  as yours, tagged internal, and the season report counts them separately.
- **No settlement fee on this rail.** Through Circle Gateway the price in the 402 is the
  whole cost. Our other rail on Arc Mainnet, the EIP-3009 facilitator on
  [/arc](https://a-identity.xyz/arc), adds a settlement fee for the gas we pay, so the same tool
  costs more there; [/api/facilitator/status](https://a-identity.xyz/api/facilitator/status?network=eip155:5042)
  states the fee.
