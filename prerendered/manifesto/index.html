Manifesto

How should autonomous agents earn the right to act?

Identity enables autonomy. Authority defines its limits. Accountability makes it trustworthy.

Revised 2026-10-22One trust layer. Many ecosystems.

Software now buys data, calls tools and pays for work on its own. The question is no longer whether agents will act. It is what gives an action standing: who did it, for whom, within what limits, and what remains once it is done.

This page is our answer, written for investors, grant programs, partners and researchers as much as for developers. The laws are short on purpose. The mechanism under each one is how we keep it, and the landscape section places us among the other answers being built in 2026.

01The four pillars

An action passes through four questions.

Identity, authority, boundaries and accountability. The gate in the middle answers ALLOW, WARN or DENY, and the receipt outlives the action that produced it.

  1. 01Passport
    Identity
    Who is the agent?

    A verifiable passport, read live from a registry, not a name typed into a form.

  2. 02Mandate
    Authority
    On whose behalf?

    A human sets the mandate. The agent inherits it and cannot widen it.

  3. 03Policy
    Boundaries
    What may it do?

    Caps, an allowlist and a freeze switch, enforced where the money sits.

  4. The gate
    ALLOWWARNDENY
    • ALLOW continues to settlement.
    • WARN waits for a human to approve.
    • DENY signs nothing, and says why.
  5. 04Receipt
    Accountability
    What survives?

    A receipt and a provenance record, so responsibility outlives the moment.

  6. Receipts feed the trust graph. The next check reads them.

02Ten principles

Ten laws, each with the part of the product that keeps it.

Written to last the way simple laws do, and tied to a mechanism so that none of them stays a slogan.

  1. 01

    Identity must be verifiable.

    A claim of identity that nobody can check is a costume, and software wears costumes well.

    In the product

    An ERC-8004 passport read live from the registry, plus KYA, which proves the agent controls the wallet it pays from.

  2. 02

    Authority must be explicit.

    Acting for someone is a mandate, not an assumption. A mandate can be read, and read back.

    In the product

    The vault separates an owner role from an operator role. The owner sets and signs the policy; the operator key may only pay inside it, and a payment past the caps reverts on-chain. Only the owner can pay past them, and the receipt says so.

  3. 03

    Permission must have boundaries.

    Open-ended permission is a key left in the door. A boundary is what makes delegation safe to give.

    In the product

    Daily caps, an auto-approve line, payee allowlists and freeze, enforced in the server pre-check, the on-chain vault and the Circle wallet screening.

  4. 04

    Humans must retain meaningful control.

    Autonomy is delegated. What is delegated can be paused, narrowed or taken back, by a person, at any time.

    In the product

    The owner freezes, overrides, withdraws and resets limits. On Stellar pubnet the console prepares those calls for the owner to sign from their own wallet, so the server never holds that key; the EVM mainnet vaults are still owned by our operating wallet, and the proof pages say so.

  5. 05

    Actions must be attributable.

    Every action should answer three questions afterwards: who did it, for whom, and under which mandate.

    In the product

    Each settlement is a transaction hash with a payer and a payee. The provenance ledger labels our own payers internal, so a demo never reads as demand.

  6. 06

    Trust must be contextual.

    The same agent deserves a different answer for a cent than for a thousand dollars, and for a stranger than for a known payee.

    In the product

    The pre-action check answers ALLOW, WARN or DENY against the amount, the payee and the policy, with the reasons attached.

  7. 07

    Autonomy must not remove accountability.

    Speed is not an excuse. The record has to be as fast as the act, or the act should wait.

    In the product

    Nothing is marked settled without a receipt carrying a matching transfer log. A payment we cannot prove is reported unproven.

  8. 08

    Trust should be portable.

    A reputation locked inside one platform is a hostage, not an asset. It should travel with the agent.

    In the product

    A deterministic 0 to 1000 score computed from on-chain settlements, validation and tenure. Same inputs, same number, for anyone who recomputes it.

  9. 09

    Infrastructure should remain interoperable.

    No single vendor should own the right to act. Standards outlive products, and so should the standing of an agent.

    In the product

    ERC-8004 for identity, x402 for payment, MCP for connection. A chain joins by declaring one registry entry, not by a rewrite.

  10. 10

    Complexity should remain invisible.

    The safeguards should cost the user one decision, not a course. Safety that is hard to use gets switched off.

    In the product

    One rule for a buyer: verify first, then pay. One pre-check call returns the verdict and the reasons.

03From principle to mechanism

Five words of the manifesto, five parts of the product.

Identity, authority, decision, evidence and network trust each name one thing you can call, read or verify today.

PrincipleMechanismThe standard behind it
Identity
Passportthe ERC-8004 identity plus KYA

The ERC-8004 Identity registry, read live on every EVM chain in our registry that carries one, and the Validation registry only where we have wired it, two testnets today; elsewhere a KYA result is verified off-chain and recorded, not anchored. KYA is a revocable wallet-control attestation, not an audit of behavior.

Authority
Mandatethe spend policy a human sets

The AgentSpendPolicy vault, Solidity on EVM chains and Soroban on Stellar. Owner and operator are separate roles: an operator payment past the caps reverts on-chain, only the owner can pay past them, and the receipt records which key paid.

Decision
Gatewaythe pre-action check

pre_action_check over MCP answers ALLOW, WARN or DENY with reasons. The same verdict is sold per call over x402 as a paid trust check.

Evidence
Receiptsettlement records and the provenance ledger

A settlement counts only when its receipt carries the matching transfer log. The ledger behind the proof pages lists every artifact per rail, with explorer links derived rather than typed.

Network trust
Trust graphthe deterministic 0 to 1000 reputation

A pure function of settlements, validation, tenure, behavior and discipline, with a 90-day half-life on volume. Published in full, so anyone can recompute it.

05What we refuse

Four things we will not do.

Each one is a line the code enforces, not a promise in a document.

  • No autonomous key custody.

    Where the code says no: Keys are user-held or gated behind a credential the operator sets; a missing credential is a labeled no-op, never a mock that looks real. Where a vault is owner-signed, Stellar pubnet today, the server never sees that key; where it is not, the ledger names the server wallet as owner.

  • No agent moves value beyond the caps a human set.

    Where the code says no: The server pre-check refuses first. The vault reverts an agent payment past the caps whatever the server said, and the only key that can pay past them belongs to the owner, on the record. The Circle wallet screening is the third gate.

  • No claim of settlement without a receipt.

    Where the code says no: A payment counts only once its receipt carries the matching transfer log. Until then it is reported as unproven, and nothing on the site calls it settled.

  • No number on the site that cannot be reproduced from a live read.

    Where the code says no: Counters are read from the settlement logs on every request, our own payers are labeled internal, and a zero is published as a zero rather than dressed up.

Autonomous systems should be free to act, but never free from identity, authority or accountability.
